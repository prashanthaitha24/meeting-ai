Shapes all around us

Shapes are everywhere — a wheel is a circle, a window is a square, a slice of pizza looks like a triangle. Learning the basic shapes helps you notice patterns in the world and is the start of geometry. This lesson introduces the first shapes to know and how to tell them apart.

The first shapes to know

Here are three shapes you will see all the time. A circle is perfectly round with no corners. A square has four straight sides that are all the same length, and four corners. A triangle has three straight sides and three corners.

The easiest way to tell shapes apart is to count the sides and corners. No corners means a circle. Three corners means a triangle. Four equal sides means a square.

A few more shapes

There are more shapes to meet. A rectangle is like a square but longer on two sides — think of a door or a phone. A star has points sticking out. Each shape has its own number of sides and corners, and once you know them you will start spotting them everywhere.

Look around the room right now: how many circles, squares, and rectangles can you find?

Shapes in the world

Shapes aren't just in books — they make up real things. Wheels and clocks are circles. Windows and picture frames are often squares or rectangles. A slice of pizza or a roof can look like a triangle.

Noticing shapes in everyday objects is a fun game, and it helps you understand how things are built and how they fit together.

Check yourself

Try these. Pick an answer to see whether it's right and why.

How many corners does a triangle have?
A triangle has three straight sides and three corners.
Which shape is perfectly round with no corners?
A circle is round all the way around and has no corners at all.
A square has how many sides that are the same length?
A square has four straight sides, all the same length, and four corners.
A window shaped longer than it is tall is most like which shape?
A rectangle has longer sides on two edges, like a door or a wide window.
What is the best way to tell shapes apart?
Counting the sides and corners tells you which shape it is — 3 corners is a triangle, no corners is a circle.

In a nutshell

  • A circle is round with no corners; a square has 4 equal sides; a triangle has 3 sides.
  • Tell shapes apart by counting their sides and corners.
  • Shapes are everywhere — wheels are circles, windows are squares or rectangles, roofs can be triangles.
