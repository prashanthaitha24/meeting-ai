Telling the time on a clock

Clocks tell us what time it is, so we know when to eat, play, and sleep. A clock face can look confusing with its two hands and twelve numbers, but there is a simple system behind it. This lesson shows what each hand does and how to read o'clock and half past.

The two hands

A clock face has the numbers 1 to 12 around it and two hands that point to them. The short, thick hand is the hour hand — it tells you the hour. The long, thin hand is the minute hand — it tells you the minutes. The minute hand moves faster and goes all the way round in one hour.

A good way to remember: the short hand is small like the word 'hour', and the long hand travels a long way to count the minutes.

Reading o'clock

When the minute hand points straight up to the 12, it is exactly on the hour — we say o'clock. To read it, just look at where the hour hand points. If the hour hand is on the 3 and the minute hand is on the 12, it is 3 o'clock.

So the two-step rule for o'clock is: check the long hand is on 12, then read the number the short hand points to.

Worked example
The long hand points to 12 and the short hand points to 8. What time is it?
  1. The long (minute) hand is on 12, so it is exactly o'clock.
  2. Read where the short (hour) hand points: the 8.
  3. So the time is 8 o'clock.
Answer: 8 o'clock

Half past

When the minute hand points straight down to the 6, half an hour has passed — we say half past. The hour hand will be halfway between two numbers. If the minute hand is on 6 and the hour hand is between 3 and 4, it is half past 3.

Later you'll learn quarter past and quarter to, and how each number the minute hand passes counts 5 minutes. But o'clock and half past are the two most important times to know first, and they cover a big part of the day.
