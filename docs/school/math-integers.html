Integers: numbers below zero

Numbers don't stop at zero. When the temperature drops below freezing, or you owe money, you need numbers less than zero — negative numbers. Together with zero and the counting numbers, they make up the integers. This lesson shows what negative numbers mean and how to add and subtract with them using the number line.

The number line goes both ways

The integers are the whole numbers and their negatives: … −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3 … A negative number is less than zero, written with a minus sign in front, like −2. On the number line, positive numbers go right of zero and negative numbers go left of zero.

Negative numbers are real and useful: a temperature of −5°C is 5 degrees below freezing, and a balance of −$10 means you owe $10. The further left on the line, the smaller the number, so −5 is smaller than −2.

Adding and subtracting on the line

The number line makes adding and subtracting integers easy: adding moves you right, and subtracting moves you left. Start at the first number and step the right way. For −2 + 5, start at −2 and move 5 steps right, landing on 3.

For 3 − 5, start at 3 and move 5 steps left, passing zero and landing on −2. Whenever you subtract more than you have, you cross zero into the negatives.

Worked example
Work out −2 + 5.
  1. Start at −2 on the number line.
  2. Adding means move right, so step 5 to the right.
  3. −1, 0, 1, 2, 3 — you land on 3.
Answer: −2 + 5 = 3

Two minuses and opposites

Every number has an opposite the same distance from zero on the other side: the opposite of 4 is −4. Adding a number and its opposite gives zero (4 + (−4) = 0).

One rule to remember: subtracting a negative is the same as adding. Taking away a debt is like gaining money, so 5 − (−3) = 5 + 3 = 8. Two minus signs together turn into a plus.

Check yourself

Try these. Pick an answer to see whether it's right and why.

Which number is smaller, −5 or −2?
On the number line −5 is further left than −2, so −5 is the smaller number.
On the number line, adding moves you which way?
Adding moves you to the right (toward bigger numbers); subtracting moves you left.
What is −2 + 5?
Start at −2 and move 5 right: −1, 0, 1, 2, 3. So −2 + 5 = 3.
What is 3 − 5?
Start at 3 and move 5 left, crossing zero to land on −2.
What is 5 − (−3)?
Subtracting a negative is the same as adding: 5 − (−3) = 5 + 3 = 8.

In a nutshell

  • Integers include negatives: numbers less than zero, written with a minus sign, sitting left of zero on the line.
  • Adding moves right on the number line; subtracting moves left, and you can cross zero into the negatives.
  • Every number has an opposite (4 and −4) that sum to zero.
  • Subtracting a negative is the same as adding: 5 − (−3) = 8.
