A timeline from grade 9 to submit

Applications aren't won in senior fall — they're built over four years of ordinary choices. Here's a calm, year-by-year plan so the process feels like steady steps, not a last-minute scramble.

On this page

Every college keeps its own deadlines and requirements, and policies (like test-optional rules) change year to year. Use this as a framework, and confirm the specifics for your schools and country. Dates below assume the US calendar; adjust for your system.

Grade 9 — build the foundation

  • Take school seriously from day one. Freshman grades are part of your transcript; a strong start makes everything later easier.
  • Try things broadly, then notice what sticks. This is the year to explore activities and find one or two you genuinely care about.
  • Build good habits — study routines, reading, time management. They compound.
  • Don't stress about colleges yet. The best "admissions work" in grade 9 is simply doing well and finding real interests.

Grade 10 — go deeper

  • Deepen your commitments. Move from trying activities to taking on more responsibility in the one or two that matter to you — this is where a "spike" starts.
  • Step up course rigour where you can, in subjects you're strong in.
  • Start a loose brag sheet — a running note of what you've done and achieved. You'll be grateful for it senior year.
  • Consider a first practice PSAT/SAT late in the year just to see the format — low stakes, purely diagnostic.

Grade 11 — the pivotal year — the one that matters most

Junior year is the most important: it's your last full year of grades before you apply, and the year most testing and college research happens.

  • Take your most rigorous, best-performing year. These grades weigh heavily.
  • Test with intention. Prep efficiently and sit the SAT/ACT, leaving room to retake once if needed. Our SAT guide and free practice are built for exactly this.
  • Take on leadership or real impact in your main activities.
  • Research colleges and start a rough list across reach / match / likely schools. Visit or explore online; note what genuinely appeals.
  • Build teacher relationships — the teachers who'll write your recommendations should know you well by spring.
  • Late spring: ask two teachers for recommendations, and start brainstorming essay ideas over the summer.

Grade 12 — apply

Senior fall is execution, not discovery — if the earlier years are done, this is manageable.

Keep a per-school checklist — essays, supplements, recommendations, test scores, aid forms — because the reason applications go wrong at this stage is a missed piece, not weak content.

Money & financial aid

For most families this matters as much as admission. Don't leave it to the end:

  • Learn the aid forms early (in the US, the FAFSA, and the CSS Profile for some schools) and their deadlines — they're often separate from and earlier than you'd expect.
  • Search scholarships throughout junior and senior year, not just at the end.
  • Understand net price, not sticker price. A pricier school with strong aid can cost a family less than a cheaper one — use each college's net price calculator.
  • Apply to a range including affordable, likely-admit options so you have a genuine, affordable choice in spring.

Principles for every year

  • Grades and rigour first. The transcript is the biggest factor; protect it every year.
  • Depth beats breadth. Two real commitments over four years beat a pile of clubs.
  • Keep records as you go. A running brag sheet turns senior-year panic into copy-and-paste.
  • Steady beats frantic. A little each year makes senior fall calm; nothing until then makes it brutal.
  • Fit over prestige. Aim for schools where you'll thrive and that you can afford, not just the most famous names.
